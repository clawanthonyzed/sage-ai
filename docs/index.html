Midwife Wisdom · 15 May 2026

5 things your midwife wishes she had time to tell you.

She does know. She just had 6 other patients, a ward meeting, and approximately 4 minutes with you at that appointment. Here's everything she wanted to say:

  1. Your birth preferences matter — write them down. A one-page document. Your midwife will read it. Give her the chance.
  2. 'Overdue' is a range, not a deadline. 40 weeks is an average. Most first-time parents go to 41 weeks. You have time.
  3. You can ask for more time. For any decision. "Can I have 10 minutes to think about this?" The answer is always yes.
  4. Your partner needs a job. Give them one before you go in. "Speak to staff on my behalf when I'm in labour" — that's a real job that helps.
  5. The first hour after birth is yours. Skin to skin. No rush. Say it out loud to your midwife when you arrive.

Labour Education · 16 May 2026

The labour wall — and why it means you're almost there.

Almost every birthing person hits it. Almost nobody warns you it's coming.

It's the moment — usually late active labour, early transition — where everything suddenly feels impossible. Your brain says: something is wrong. I cannot do this.

Here's what's actually happening: your body is flooding with oxytocin. Your cervix is doing the hardest work it will ever do. You are almost certainly 7–8cm dilated.

Impossible is the signal that you are almost there.

That is not a flaw in the design. That is the design. Write it down. Tell your birth partner. When it feels like too much — that feeling is almost done. 🌿

Postpartum · 19 May 2026

The fourth trimester: what nobody tells you about the first 12 weeks.

Your baby was born. But so were you. As a mother. And that birth deserves the same reverence.

The fourth trimester is weeks 1–12 after birth. It belongs to you and your baby. Here's what I want every new mother to know:

  • Rest is not laziness — it is the work. Your body is healing from the inside out. Let it.
  • Your hormones will do things. Day 3–5 after birth, most women cry for no reason. This is your milk coming in and your hormones recalibrating. It is not depression. It passes.
  • You don't have to see anyone. Not at 6 weeks. Not at 10 weeks. "We're not ready" is a complete sentence.
  • Feed your body like you're training for something hard. Because you are. Warm food. Protein. Water. Lots of it.
  • The nights don't last forever. They just feel like they do. Every single parent comes out the other side saying the same thing: it went faster than I thought.

Birth Partners

Learn what transition looks like so you don't panic when she says she can't do this. That's when she's almost done. Your job: stay calm and believe harder than she can right now.

Breech at 36 Weeks

Ask about ECV. Ask about your hospital's breech birth policy. Get names of practitioners who support both options. You have more choices than you're being told.

Induction

Ask BRAIN: Benefits, Risks, Alternatives, Intuition, No (what if we wait). A good team will welcome every question. Take the time you need before consenting.

Pain Relief

Gas is underrated. An epidural is not failure. A TENS machine in early labour is genuinely useful. You don't need to decide now. Keep every option open until you're in it.
