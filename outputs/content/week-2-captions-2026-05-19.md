## POST 7 — Tuesday 20 May (11:00 AM AEST)
**Image:** sage-portrait-day24-v2.png (outdoor golden hour)

The question I get more than any other:

*"Should I write a birth plan?"*

Yes. Absolutely yes. But not because it will go to plan.

A birth plan isn't a contract. It's a conversation starter. It tells your midwife who you are before you're in the middle of it and can't explain.

Here's what to include:
→ Your values (quiet and calm / active and moving / partner-led support)
→ Pain relief preferences (open to everything / prefer no epidural unless requested)
→ Who can be in the room
→ What you want for the first hour (skin to skin, delayed cord clamping, dim lights)
→ Your caesarean preferences — even if you're not planning one

One page. Clear language. Give it to your midwife when you arrive.

She will read it. I promise. 📋

Things you are allowed to do in labour (that nobody explicitly tells you):

✓ Ask your midwife to wait outside while you think
✓ Request a different midwife if something feels off
✓ Change your mind about pain relief — at any point
✓ Ask for more time before any intervention
✓ Say no to students in the room
✓ Ask exactly what is about to happen before it happens
✓ Have your support person stay during all procedures unless safety requires otherwise
✓ Ask "is this an emergency, or do we have time to discuss?"

*You have rights in labour. Use them.*

## POST 10 — Friday 23 May (6:00 AM AEST)
**Image:** sage-portrait-day24-v1.png

Breastfeeding week 1 truths no one told me as a first-time midwife:

1. Day 2–4 is the hardest. Your milk is coming in, your nipples aren't used to this, your baby is learning. It doesn't mean it's not working.

2. Colostrum is enough. It's designed to be exactly what a newborn needs in tiny amounts. Trust the design.

3. Latch problems are common and fixable. Ask for a lactation consultant consult before you leave hospital — don't wait until you're home and it's 2am.

4. Nipple pain that doesn't improve after the first 30 seconds of feeding = latch issue. It shouldn't hurt throughout.

5. Fed is best. If breastfeeding doesn't work for you — for any reason — you are still an incredible mother. The decision is yours. Not anyone else's.

DM me if you're struggling. I've helped hundreds of families with this. 💛
